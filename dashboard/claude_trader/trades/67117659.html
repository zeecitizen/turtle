Why it didn't make money

1. 66 cents short of the target

Had gold touched 4146.92, the EA would have banked half the position (+$1,070) and trailed the rest. The low was 4147.58. The support level itself (the 17:45 swing low) attracted buyers just above it — a common pattern: the bounce starts a little before the level.

2. A conflicting setup candle

The setup was a pin bar with a long lower wick — buyers rejecting lower prices — traded as a SELL because it closed red. The EA's rules allow it (the EMA and the close decide the side), but the wick told the opposite story, and the next hours rallied hard.

3. The breakeven step did its job

Without the 75% breakeven step this trade would have gone back to its full stop (4166.76): gold reached 4168.69 by 03:00, which would have been −$1,828. The rule turned a likely full loss into $0.

One-off or a pattern? The full-year check

Over the full year, trades that reached the 75% breakeven level but not TP1 end at about $0 by design. This was the EA working as designed on a day the move reversed just before the target.

What would have prevented it

Nothing to fix here — the trade risked $1,828 and lost nothing. A closer target would have banked a profit on this trade, but report.md §12 tested earlier/partial exits and they cost more than they saved over the year.
